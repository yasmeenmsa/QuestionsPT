1014 Unit 3 Examinations Answer
PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 90
Which recommendation would likely be the MOST beneficial to
diminish the severity of the patient’s current symptoms?

1. Reduce cell phone use

2. Lower the height of the computer keyboard

3. Wear gloves when exposed to cold

4. Increase dietary iron intake

Correct Answer: 1 (Dutton p. 786)

De Quervain’s tenosynovitis is typically caused by repetitive

activities involving thumb abduction and extension. Reducing

the frequency of activities stressing the abductor pollicis longus
and extensor pollicis brevis muscles can significantly reduce
inflammation and decrease the patient’s symptoms.

1. A therapist should advise a patient with de Quervain’s
tenosynovitis to reduce their cell phone use to limit
unnecessary stress to the affected structures. Cell phone
use commonly involves repetitive use of the thumb, such
as when typing or scrolling. Additionally, holding a cell
phone can place a stress on the abductor pollicis longus and
extensor pollicis brevis muscles.

2. Advising a patient to lower the height of the computer
keyboard may provide some relief from de Quervain’s
tenosynovitis symptoms, though this recommendation is
more likely to benefit individuals with a cervical pathology,
such as an upper trapezius strain due to improper ergonomics
at their workstation.

3. Advising a patient to wear gloves when exposed to cold
is more likely to benefit an individual with Raynaud’s
phenomenon. Symptoms associated with de Quervain’s
tenosynovitis are not likely to be changed by wearing gloves
in the cold.

4. Increasing dietary iron intake is not likely to impact the
symptoms of de Quervain’s tenosynovitis. A therapist is more
likely to advise a patient with anemia to increase dietary iron
intake.

PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 91

A physical therapist completes

the upper extremity resisted 4

test depicted in the video

on a patient with suspected
cervical spine pathology.
Which myotome would BEST
be assessed using this testing

procedure?

1. C4

2. ‘C5

3. C6

4. C7

Correct Answer: 2 (Dutton p. 1176)

Resisted isometric movements are designed to determine the

role of contractile tissue in an injury. The therapist attempts to

elicit a strong, static, voluntary muscle contraction. The therapist
then classifies the movement as strong or weak and painful or
pain-free.

1. The C4 myotome is commonly assessed by examining the
diaphragm. The therapist uses a tape measure to quantify the
amount of rib expansion that occurs with a deep breath. This
measurement is then compared to the same measurement
performed with the patient at rest. The C4 myotome can also
be assessed by providing manual resistance after the patient
completes a shoulder shrug.

2. The C5 myotome is commonly assessed by performing
resisted isometric movements of the shoulder abductors
or the shoulder external rotators. The image shows the
therapist assessing the shoulder abductors by applying
downward force on the humerus while the patient resists
the movement. The deltoid and supraspinatus muscles are
the primary muscles active during this resisted test.

3. The C6 myotome is commonly assessed by performing
resisted isometric movements of the elbow flexors or
shoulder internal rotators.

4. The C7 myotome is commonly assessed by performing
resisted isometric movements of the elbow extensors or
wrist flexors.
