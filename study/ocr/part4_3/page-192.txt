PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 178
Which finding would be MOST probable when performing a valgus
stress test to the right knee?

1. Pain with no discernible laxity when compared to the
contralateral limb

2. Pain with discernible laxity when compared to the
contralateral limb

3. No pain with discernible laxity when compared to the
contralateral limb

4. No pain with no discernible laxity when compared to the
contralateral limb

Correct Answer: 1 (Magee p. 28)

The valgus stress test is used to test for injury to the medial

collateral ligament of the knee. A grade sprain would be

characterized by mild pain and swelling and little to no damage of
the ligament fibers.

1. Since the injury occurred two days ago, the patient will
likely have pain with testing. A grade sprain has little to no
damage of the ligament fibers. As a result, there will be no
discernible laxity when compared to the contralateral side.

2. Although the patient would have pain with testing, there will
be no discernible laxity compared to the contralateral limb.
Joint play would more likely be excessive with a grade Ill
sprain.

3. The patient will likely have pain with testing since the injury
occurred two days ago. Additionally, there will not be
discernible laxity since the injury was only a grade sprain.

4. There will be no discernible laxity compared to the
contralateral side. However, pain will likely be present given
the acuity of the injury.

PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 179
Which statement BEST summarizes the patient’s right knee range of
motion compared to normal values?

1. Decreased knee extension, increased knee flexion

2. Decreased knee extension, normal knee flexion

3. Increased knee extension, increased knee flexion

4. Increased knee extension, normal knee flexion

Correct Answer: 4 (Norkin p. 315)

Physical therapists should be familiar with normal values for range

of motion so they can accurately identify when a limitation exists.

The documented passive range of motion at the knee for this

patient is 7 degrees of hyperextension and 138 degrees of flexion.

Normal passive knee range of motion according to the American

Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons is 0-140 degrees.

1. The patient has increased knee extension, not decreased. The
patient’s knee flexion value falls within the normal range and
would not be considered increased.

2. The patient has increased knee extension, not decreased. The
patient’s knee flexion value falls within the normal range.

3. The patient has increased knee extension. In adults, knee
extension values greater than 5 degrees are considered
excessive and termed genu recurvatum. The patient’s knee
flexion value falls within the normal range.

4. The patient has increased knee extension (i.e., greater than
5 degrees) and normal knee flexion.
