PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 88
Which special test is MOST likely to be positive based on the
patient’s medical diagnosis?

1. Finkelstein’s test

2. Froment’s sign

3. Murphy’s sign

4. Phalen’s test

Correct Answer: 1 (Magee p. 533)

De Quervain’s tenosynovitis is typically caused by repetitive

activities involving thumb abduction and extension, such as

racquet sports and repeated heavy lifting. As a result, special
tests targeting this medical condition typically stress the abductor
pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis muscles.

1. Finkelstein’s test is performed with the patient positioned in
sitting or standing. The patient is asked to make a fist with
the thumb tucked inside the fingers. The therapist stabilizes
the patient’s forearm and ulnarly deviates the wrist.
positive test is indicated by pain over the abductor pollicis
longus and extensor pollicis brevis tendons at the wrist and
may be indicative of de Quervain’s tenosynovitis.

2. Froment’s sign can be seen by asking a patient in sitting
or standing to hold a piece of paper between their thumb
and index finger. A positive test is indicated by the patient
flexing the distal phalanx of the thumb due to adductor
pollicis muscle paralysis. If at the same time the patient
hyperextends the metacarpophalangeal joint of the thumb,
it is termed Jeanne’s sign. Both objective findings may be
indicative of ulnar nerve compromise or paralysis.

3. Murphy’s sign can be seen by asking the patient to make
fist when positioned in sitting or standing. A positive test is
indicated by the patient’s third metacarpal remaining level
with the second and fourth metacarpals. A positive test may
be indicative of a dislocated lunate.

4. Phalen’s test is performed with the patient positioned in
sitting or standing. The therapist flexes the patient’s wrists
maximally and asks the patient to hold the position for 60
seconds. A positive test is indicated by tingling in the thumb,
index finger, middle finger, and lateral half of the ring finger
and may be indicative of carpal tunnel syndrome due to
median nerve compression.

PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 89
Which type of splint would MOST likely be prescribed for this
patient?

1. Ulnar gutter splint

2. Thumb spica splint

3. Radial gutter splint

4. Dorsal forearm splint

Correct Answer: 2 (Tan p. 222)

There are a variety of splinting options for the forearm and hand

including thumb spica, ulnar gutter, radial gutter, dorsal forearm,

volar forearm, sugar tong, and long arm splints. Splints can be
used to promote rest in the affected areas.

1. Anulnar gutter splint is a rigid splint that covers the ulnar side
of the forearm and hand as well as the fourth and fifth digits.
This type of splint is used to immobilize the metacarpals
and phalanges and is commonly used following a fracture to
these structures. An ulnar gutter splint would not be used to
immobilize the thumb.

2. A thumb spica splint is a rigid splint that covers the radial
side of the forearm and hand as well as the thumb. The splint
may cover the entire thumb or may stop at the proximal
phalanx of the thumb, and thus allow for interphalangeal
joint motion. This type of splint is used to immobilize
the wrist and metacarpophalangeal joint of the thumb.
It is commonly used for treating gamekeeper’s thumb,
scaphoid fractures, first metacarpal fractures, de Quervain’s
tenosynovitis, and other thumb injuries.

3. A radial gutter splint is a rigid splint that covers the radial
side of the forearm and hand as well as the second and
third digits. The splint includes a thenar hole to allow for
free movement of the thumb. This type of splint is used to
immobilize the metacarpals and phalanges and is commonly
used following a fracture to these structures. It would not be
the most effective option for a patient with de Quervain’s
tenosynovitis.

4. Adorsal forearm splint is a rigid splint that extends from the
proximal forearm to the metacarpal heads, allowing for full
elbow and metacarpophalangeal joint motion. The splint
includes a thenar hole to allow for free movement of the
thumb. This type of splint is used to immobilize the wrist joint
and is commonly used for treating fractures of the carpals,
fractures of the distal radius or ulna or soft tissue conditions
(e.g., sprain, tendonitis). It would not be the most effective
option for a patient with de Quervain’s tenosynovitis.
