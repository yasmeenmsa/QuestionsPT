PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 176
The patient has been lethargic since being placed on the prescribed
medication. What is the PRIMARY purpose of this medication?

1. Decrease agitation

2. Prevent seizures

3. Reduce spasticity

4. Limit arrhythmias

Correct Answer: 2 (Ciccone p. 119)

Physical therapists must possess an awareness of commonly

used pharmacological agents, their indications, and potential

side effects. Failure to recognize anticipated side effects from

medication may significantly jeopardize patient safety.

1. The most common side effect of Luminal (phenobarbital)
is sedation. As a result, agitation is typically diminished.
However, the primary purpose of the medication is to prevent
seizures.

2. Phenobarbital is classified as a barbiturate and is prescribed
most often to prevent adult seizures. Side effects include
sedation, vitamin deficiencies, nystagmus, and ataxia.

3. There are many medications that treat spasticity, but the
most common include baclofen, diazepam, and dantrolene
sodium.

4. Antiarrhythmic drugs are typically classified into four groups:
sodium channel blockers, beta-blockers, drugs that prolong
repolarization, and calcium channel blockers.

PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 177
What stimulus would have been used to identify the presence of
clonus?

1. Forcefully flex the toes

2. Rapidly dorsiflex the ankle

3. Squeeze the calf

4. Stroke the medial border of the foot

Correct Answer: 2 (O’Sullivan p. 143)

Clonus refers to rhythmic oscillation of a body part resulting from

a quick stretch. The test is ideally performed by providing a stretch

to the plantar flexors with the gastrocnemius in a relaxed position.

1. By flexing the toes, the toe extensors are placed on stretch.
These muscles are not typically spastic. This procedure would
not be an appropriate method to test for clonus.

2. Clonus is characterized by a cyclical, spasmodic alternation
of muscular contraction and relaxation in response to
a sustained stretch of a spastic muscle. It is a form of
hypertonicity and is indicative of an upper motor neuron
lesion. Dorsiflexing the ankle would place the ankle plantar
flexors on stretch. This is an appropriate method to test
for clonus since the plantar flexors are often spastic in the
presence of an upper motor neuron lesion.

3. Squeezing a muscle would not be an appropriate method to
test for clonus. The muscle should instead be rapidly placed
on stretch.

4. Stroking the skin is a method used to test the superficial
reflexes (e.g., cremasteric) and would not be an appropriate
method to test for clonus.
