PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 171
A physical therapist works with a patient who is in the initial stages
of recovery post traumatic brain injury. Based on the typical pattern
of spasticity experienced with upper motor neuron disorders, which
of the following muscles would MOST likely be affected?

1. Flexor carpi radialis

2. Supinator

3. Triceps

4. Extensor digitorum

Correct Answer: 1 (O’Sullivan p. 144)

Spasticity is a hypertonic motor disorder characterized by

velocity-dependent resistance to passive stretch. Spasticity arises

when corticospinal pathways become injured. The condition
is commonly observed in the presence of upper motor neuron
disorders. The typical pattern of spasticity for the upper extremity
includes scapula retraction and downward rotation, shoulder
adduction and internal rotation, elbow flexion, forearm pronation,
wrist flexion and adduction, finger flexion, and thumb adduction.

1. The pattern of spasticity for the upper extremity includes
flexion of the wrist. The flexor carpi radialis is one of the
muscles affected by spasticity and serves to maintain the
wrist in a flexed position.

2. The pattern of spasticity for the upper extremity includes
pronation of the forearm. The muscles typically affected are
the pronator teres and pronator quadratus, not the supinator.

3. The pattern of spasticity for the upper extremity includes
flexion of the elbow. The muscles typically affected are the
biceps, brachialis, and brachioradialis, not the triceps.

4. The pattern of spasticity for the upper extremity includes
flexion of the fingers. The muscle typically affected is the
flexor digitorum profundus, not the extensor digitorum.

PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 172
A physical therapist instructs a patient to
perform a squat exercise. The therapist
then decides to modify the exercise by
adding an additional piece of equipment
as demonstrated in the video. What is the
PRIMARY purpose of this progression?

1. Adds resistance to the exercise

2. Challenges the individual’s balance

3. Adds stability to the exercise

4. Challenges the individual’s ankle
flexibility

Correct Answer: 2 (Hoogenboom p. 387)

The video shows a patient performing a squat exercise while

standing on a BOSU ball. Performing this exercise on a BOSU ball

is a modification that should make the exercise more challenging
since the ball serves as an unstable surface.

1. Standing on a BOSU ball would have no influence on the
resistance involved in the depicted activity. Resistance
could be added by having the patient hold a weight while
performing the squat.

2. Standing on a BOSU ball requires greater balance as
compared to standing on flat ground. The addition of
the BOSU ball to standing exercises is primarily used to
challenge an individual’s balance.

3. Standing on a BOSU ball would not add stability to the
exercise. In fact, the addition of this piece of equipment
would make the exercise less stable since the individual
would have to continually adjust to the compliant surface.

4. Standing on a BOSU ball may pose an additional challenge
to an individual with limited ankle flexibility. However, the
addition of the BOSU ball challenges an individual’s balance
more than ankle flexibility.
