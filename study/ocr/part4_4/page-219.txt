PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 44
What should the physical therapist have the patient do to facilitate
awareness of using the diaphragm?
1. Flex at the waist while standing
2. Perform a sit-up
3. Huff
4. Sniff
Correct Answer: 4 (Hillegass p. 634)
Physical therapists commonly teach diaphragmatic breathing
to patients in an effort to correct abnormal chest wall motion,
decrease the work of breathing, decrease accessory muscle
activity, reduce dyspnea, increase the efficiency of breathing, and
improve the distribution of ventilation.
1. Flexing at the hip will enlist the muscles that flex the trunk,
but not the diaphragm.
2. Performing a sit-up will enlist the muscles that flex the trunk,
but not the diaphragm.
3. is a cough assistance technique used to aid in expelling
bronchial secretions.
4. Sniffing enlists the diaphragm and is useful as a training
technique to promote diaphragmatic breathing.

PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 45

What is the MOST appropriate feedback when instructing the patient
how to perform the intended breathing technique described in the
scenario?

1. Breathe in slowly through your mouth while your abdomen
moves out against your hand

2. Breathe in slowly through your nose while your abdomen
moves out against your hand

3. Breathe in slowly through your mouth while your chest moves
out against your hand

4. Breathe in slowly through your nose while your chest moves
out against your hand

Correct Answer: 2 (Frownfelter p. 360)
When teaching a patient to perform diaphragmatic breathing, the
patient is instructed to: “Place one hand on your upper chest and
the other just below your rib cage. This will allow you to feel your
diaphragm move as you breathe. Breathe in slowly through your
nose so that your abdomen moves out against your hand. The
hand on your chest should remain as still as possible. Feel your
abdomen gently rise into your hand.”

1. Diaphragmatic breathing requires the patient to breathe in
through the nose and exhale through pursed lips.

2. Diaphragmatic breathing requires the patient to breathe in
through the nose while the abdomen moves out against
hand placed over the abdomen.

3. Diaphragmatic breathing requires the patient to breathe
in through the nose and exhale through pursed lips. When
diaphragmatic breathing is performed correctly, the action
of accessory muscles and motion of the upper chest wall is
minimized during inspiration.

4. Diaphragmatic breathing requires the patient to breathe in
through the nose and exhale through pursed lips. The hand
placed on the chest should remain as still as possible when
performing diaphragmatic breathing.
