PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 87
Based on the imaging results, which of the following brain structures
would MOST likely be ischemic?

1. Occipital cortex
2. Medulla oblongata
3. Cerebellum
4. Basal ganglia
Correct Answer: 4 (Goodman Pathology p. 1472)
Blood supply is specific to particular areas within the brain. When
an artery sustains damage via occlusion or hemorrhage, there
is a specific pattern of disability that will occur. The extent of
disability is determined by the extent of occlusion, area of the
brain involved, availability of collateral circulation to the affected
area of the brain, and if the involved artery produces unilateral or
bilateral damage.

1. Blood supply to the occipital cortex is primarily provided by
the posterior cerebral artery. Occlusion of this artery can lead
to visual agnosia, homonymous hemianopsia, and cortical
blindness.

2. Blood supply to the medulla oblongata is primarily provided
by the posterior inferior cerebellar artery, which is a branch
off of the vertebral-basilar artery. Occlusion of this artery can
lead to lateral medullary syndrome.

3. Blood supply to the cerebellum is primarily provided by
various branches of the vertebral-basilar artery (i.e., posterior
inferior cerebellar, anterior inferior cerebellar, and superior
cerebellar arteries). Occlusion of these arteries can lead to
vertigo, vomiting, headache, and ataxia.

4. Blood supply to the basal ganglia is primarily provided by
the middle cerebral arteries. Occlusion of the right middle
cerebral artery can lead to aphasia, apraxia, contralateral
weakness and sensory loss, impaired spatial relations, and
impaired body schema.
