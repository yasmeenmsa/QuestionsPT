PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 149
A physical therapist performs a gait analysis on a patient with
lower extremity injury. The therapist begins the session by observing
the patient at free speed walking. What is the normal degree of toe-
out at this speed?

1. 3degrees

2. 7 degrees

3. 14 degrees

4.

Correct Answer: 2 (Levangie p. 528)

The degree of toe-out is measured by determining the angle

formed by each foot’s line of progression and a line intersecting

the center of the heel and the second toe.

1. A measurement of 3 degrees of toe-out may be associated
with walking at a relatively fast rate of speed since the
normal degree of toe-out decreases as the speed of walking
increases.

2. The degree of toe-out during free speed walking is
approximately 7 degrees.

3. Ameasurement of 14 degrees is greater than normal and may
be associated with a wide range of orthopedic or neurologic
abnormalities.

4. A measurement of 21 degrees is excessive and may be
associated with more severe orthopedic or neurologic
abnormalities.

PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 150
A physical therapist observes a patient
complete a standing squat and notes that
the patient demonstrates compensatory
movement as depicted in the video.
The therapist determines that the
compensation results from a limitation
in mobility. Based on this finding, what
muscle is MOST likely shortened?

1. Gastrocnemius
2. Popliteus
3. Soleus
4. Tibialis anterior
Correct Answer: 3 (Dutton p. 1032)
Adequate muscle length is necessary for a patient to complete
variety of functional activities. In the absence of adequate muscle
length, the patient will often employ a variety of compensations
to complete the desired task. In the video, the patient’s heels rise
from the ground as they descend into the squat, indicating that
they have a limitation in dorsiflexion mobility.

1. The gastrocnemius is a two-joint muscle that crosses both
the knee and ankle joints. When the knee is flexed, the
muscle is placed on slack which allows for normal ankle
range of motion. A limitation in dorsiflexion when the knee is
extended would more likely be associated with shortness of
the gastrocnemius and possibly the plantaris.

2. The popliteus muscle medially rotates the tibia on the femur
and flexes the knee joint in non-weight bearing. The muscle
laterally rotates the femur on the tibia and flexes the knee
joint in weight bearing. The popliteus muscle is a one-
joint muscle and would not influence the ankle during the
standing squat.

3. The soleus is a one-joint muscle that plantar flexes the ankle
joint. Shortness of the soleus muscle would be evident as the
ankle becomes increasingly dorsiflexed with the knee bent
during the standing squat. Lifting the heels from the floor is
a common compensation that allows the patient to deepen
the squat without utilizing additional ankle dorsiflexion.

4. The tibialis anterior acts to dorsiflex the ankle joint and
assists in inversion of the foot. Shortness of the muscle would
more likely influence activities requiring plantar flexion of the
ankle joint and eversion of the foot.
