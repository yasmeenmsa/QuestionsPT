HREE Answer Key CHAPTER 12 1131
PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 132
Which of the following findings would MOST likely have been
identified in the cerebrospinal fluid sample to confirm the medical
diagnosis?

1. Elevated erythrocyte levels
2. Elevated protein levels
3. Low gamma globulin levels
4. Low glucose levels
Correct Answer: 2 (Goodman Pathology p. 1655)
Lumbar puncture is an invasive procedure that is used to diagnose
problems with the spine or brain. The procedure is performed by
inserting a needle into the subarachnoid space in the lumbar spine
and drawing cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) out to be tested. Lumbar
puncture can be used to diagnose conditions such as encephalitis,
meningitis, and Guillain-Barre syndrome.

1. should have no red blood cells (i.e., erythrocytes)
and typically have low numbers of mononuclear leukocytes.
High levels of erythrocytes in the CSF are a sign of bleeding
or the result of a traumatic lumbar puncture.

2. Guillain-Barre syndrome is a temporary inflammation and
demyelination of the myelin sheaths of peripheral nerves.
CSF samples in patients diagnosed with Guillain-Barre
syndrome typically contain high protein levels (e.g., albumin).
The increase in protein is associated with the widespread
inflammation of the nerve roots. Elevated protein levels
without an increase in leukocytes is the most common finding
in CSF samples of patients with Guillain-Barre syndrome.

3. Gamma globulin is a type of blood protein produced by
lymphocytes and plasma cells during an immune response,
often referred to as immunoglobulins. Low levels of
gamma globulin would not be expected with Guillain-Barre
syndrome. However, elevated levels of gamma globulin are
commonly found in the CSF of patients diagnosed with
multiple sclerosis.

4. Low glucose levels in CSF samples may be due to hypogly-
cemia, bacterial infection (e.g., meningitis, tuberculosis) or
certain other types of meningitis.
