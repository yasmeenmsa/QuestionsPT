PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 42
Which portion of the physical therapist’s body would be in direct
contact with the patient when administering mediate percussion?

1. Palmar surface of the thumb

2. Palmar surface of the middle finger

3. Palmar surface of the hand

4. Dorsal surface of the hand

Correct Answer: 2 (Boissonnault p. 159)

Mediate percussion refers to the act of tapping the surface of

the body to identify areas of altered density. The quality of the

generated sound provides the therapist with information on lung
density.

1. The palmar surface of the thumb is not used in isolation for
any specific cardiopulmonary assessment procedure.

2. The therapist performs the assessment by placing the palmar
surface of the middle finger of one hand firmly against the
chest wall in the intercostal space parallel to the ribs while
the other fingers remain off the chest. The therapist then
strikes the middle finger with a series of quick, sharp blows
using the middle finger of the other hand.

3. The palmar surface of the hand is used to administer vibration.

4. surface of the hand is not used when administering
mediate percussion. Instead, it is commonly used to assess
temperature. The thin skin on the dorsal surface of the hand
is much more sensitive to temperature than the palm of the
hand.

PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 43
Which outcome would be the MOST appropriate to measure when
determining the patient’s endurance level using the Six-Minute Walk
Test?

1. Perceived exertion
2. Heart rate response
3. Elapsed time
4. Distance walked
Correct Answer: 4 (Paz p. 506)
The Six-Minute Walk Test is used to determine a patient’s functional
exercise capacity. The test is commonly used upon admission, at
discharge, and to monitor progress or decline throughout physical
therapy. This tool is administered to various populations, including
those with cardiac impairments, pulmonary disease, chronic
conditions, neurologic conditions, and patients recovering from
orthopedic surgical procedures. The administrator of the test
should strive to provide consistent verbal cues as guided by test
instructions and assure they are not pacing the patient.

1. The patient is instructed to walk as quickly as they can and
attempt to cover as much ground as possible within the six
minute period. The therapist does not attempt to record the
patient’s perceived exertion. However, the patient must let
the therapist know if they experience chest pain or dizziness.

2. The heart rate response will likely increase as the intensity
and duration of the test increases. However, the test is not
designed to examine the heart rate response. Heart rate,
blood pressure, oxygen saturation, and a dyspnea score are
typically assessed prior to and after the administration of the
test.

3. The elapsed time for the Six-Minute Walk Test is six minutes,
as the name implies, and therefore does not vary during the
administration of the test.

4. The test requires the therapist to measure the distance the
patient walks within a six minute period with rest periods
permitted as necessary.
