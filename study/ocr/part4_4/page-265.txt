PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 133
Which of the following signs or symptoms would have been
MOST characteristic of this medical condition during the inpatient
hospitalization?

1. Difficulty breathing
2. Spasticity
3. Cognitive deficits
4. Absent sensation
Correct Answer: 1 (Goodman Pathology p. 1655)
Guillain-Barre syndrome is a rare autoimmune disease triggered
by infection that affects the peripheral nerves and nerve roots.
There is a temporary inflammation and demyelination of the
myelin sheaths of peripheral nerves. The signs and symptoms
are motor weakness in a distal to proximal progression, sensory
impairment, and possible respiratory paralysis. Breathing may
be impacted when there is damage to the phrenic nerve, which
innervates the diaphragm.

1. Guillain-Barre syndrome can result in respiratory compro-
mise due to weakness of the respiratory muscles. Extensive
involvement of the respiratory muscles may result in the
need for mechanical ventilation.

2. Since Guillain-Barre syndrome primarily affects peripheral
nerves, flaccidity is expected.

3. Guillain-Barre syndrome is an acute polyneuropathy affecting
peripheral nerves. Given an intact central nervous system
cognitive deficits would not be anticipated.

4. A patient may experience paresthesia and numbness during
the acute stage of Guillain-Barre syndrome. However, absent
sensation is not characteristic of this disease. Sensory loss is
often mild and transient throughout the progression of the
disease process.

PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 134
Which of the following lower extremity strengthening exercises
would be the MOST appropriate given the patient’s impairments and
disease progression?

1. Gluteal and quadriceps setting exercises and active range of
motion

2. Aquatic resistance exercises with foam weights

3. Active-assisted range of motion and gravity-minimized
exercises

4. Passive range of motion, bridging, and long arc quad exercises
Correct Answer: 3 (Goodman Pathology p. 1657)
The level of disability with Guillain-Barre syndrome usually peaks
within two to four weeks after onset. Physical therapy should be
initiated upon admission to the hospital with focus on passive
range of motion, or in some cases, active-assisted range of motion,
positioning, and light exercise. During the acute stage, therapists
must limit overexertion and fatigue to avoid exacerbation of
symptoms.

1. Although isometric exercises (e.g., gluteal sets, quad sets)
are typically an appropriate method of early strengthening,
caution should be used with beginning strengthening too
soon during the acute phase. Additionally, performing active
range of motion exercises is not consistent with the patient’s
current strength of Poor (2/5).

2. Aquatic resistance exercises are a viable choice once
patient stabilizes. Since this patient is still in the ascending or
acute phase with Poor (2/5) strength in the lower extremities,
aquatic exercise would likely overexert or fatigue the patient
and exacerbate the patient’s condition.

3. Since this patient is still in the early stages, the focus of
physical therapy treatment should be on maintaining joint
range of motion, positioning, and only light exercises until
active and later resistive exercises can be initiated. Given
the patient’s current lower extremity strength of Poor (2/5),
performing active-assisted range of motion and exercise in
gravity-minimized positions is the most appropriate choice
at this time.

4. Passive range of motion should be performed during this
early stage to help maintain joint range of motion. However,
bridging and long arc quad exercises exceed the patient’s
current strength of Poor (2/5). As a result, active-assisted
range of motion and gravity-minimized exercises are the
most appropriate choice given the patient’s current status.
