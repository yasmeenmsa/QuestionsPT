PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 165
A physical therapist intends to use compression therapy as part of
a patient’s plan of care. The use of compression therapy would be
contraindicated for which of the following impairments?

1. Lower extremity edema due to congestive heart failure

2. Decreased mobility following total knee arthroplasty

3. Hypertrophic scarring following a burn to the lower
extremities

4. Residual limb edema following transfemoral amputation
Correct Answer: 1 (Hillegass p. 368)
Compression therapy is the use of external pressure on the body
to improve fluid balance and circulation or modify scar tissue
formation. Indications for the use of compression include control
of edema, prevention of deep venous thrombosis, treatment of
venous stasis ulcers, residual limb shaping after amputation, and
control of hypertrophic scarring.

1. Edema of the limbs is an indication for compression
therapy. However, in patients with congestive heart failure,
compression therapy should not be used since the movement
of fluid from the periphery back to the heart may further
increase the stress on an already failing heart.

2. Decreased mobility following total knee arthroplasty increases
the risk for deep venous thrombosis (DVT). Compression
therapy can help reduce the risk of DVT by increasing
circulation.

3. Hypertrophic scarring is a common complication of burn
injuries. Compression therapy is the most common treatment
used to control hypertrophic scar formation. Compression
garments have been shown to decrease the height and
increase the pliability of hypertrophic scars.

4. Compression therapy is commonly used after amputation to
help shape the residual limb in preparation for proper fit of
the prosthesis. Compression helps with residual limb shaping
by controlling edema.

PTEXAM THREE: QUESTION 166

A physical therapist administers a test to
assess a patient’s circulation as depicted

in the video. What is the MOST important 4
component to assess when identifying if 7

this test is positive or negative? 4

1. The amount of time taken for color
to return to the nail

2. The amount of time taken to create
blanching of the nail with direct
pressure

3. The amount of color returning to
the nail

4. The amount of blanching of the
nail present with direct pressure

Correct Answer: 1 (Magee p. 1221)

The physical therapist is performing the capillary refill test, also

known as the digital blood flow test. This test is used to assess

blood flow to the distal extremities. The examiner compresses the
nail bed, resulting in blanching of the nail bed. Color should return
to the nail bed within three seconds.

1. The amount of time taken for color to return to the nail
should be noted by the therapist. This value is used to
assess the level of blood flow to the distal extremity.

2. While the therapist should attempt to blanch the nail with
direct pressure, the amount of time it takes to do so is not
consideration when assessing this test.

3. of color returning to the nail is not a consideration
when assessing this test. It is likely that all the color will
eventually return to the nail bed. However, the time it takes
to do so will vary depending on blood flow status.

4. The amount of blanching of the nail is directly related to the
amount of pressure applied by the therapist and is not an
indicator of the patient’s blood flow status.
