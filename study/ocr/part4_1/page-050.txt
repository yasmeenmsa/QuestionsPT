PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 88
Which of the following terms BEST describes this child’s type of
cerebral palsy?

1. Monoplegic

2. Diplegic

3. Hemiplegic

4. Quadriplegic

Correct Answer: 2 (Tecklin p. 183)

Cerebral palsy is a permanent neurologic condition that results

from a defect or lesion to the immature brain. Cerebral palsy

presents with motor and possible sensory impairments that are
present at early infancy. Children with cerebral palsy can have
one or more limbs involved as well as involvement of the trunk.

Cerebral palsy is classified by the area of the body involved (e.g.,

monoplegia) and is based on the movement abnormality (e.g.,

Spastic).

1. Monoplegic cerebral palsy involves only one limb.

2. Diplegic cerebral palsy describes involvement of the lower
limbs. A crouched stance and scissored gait are indications
of spastic diplegia cerebral palsy. Botox injections in
bilateral lower extremities are often used to decrease the
effects of spasticity in this type of cerebral palsy.

3. Hemiplegic cerebral palsy involves both the upper and lower
limbs on one side of the body.

4. Quadriplegic cerebral palsy involves all four limbs.

PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 89
Which muscle group is MOST likely to be shortened based on the
patient’s clinical presentation?

1. Hip adductors

2. Knee extensors

3. Hip abductors

4. Ankle dorsiflexors

Correct Answer: 1 (Goodman Pathology

p. 1539)

Cerebral palsy is a non-progressive disorder of the central
nervous system resulting in impairments in posture and volitional
movement. Spastic diplegia describes an upper motor neuron
lesion in the motor cortex of the cerebrum involving the trunk and
lower extremities with the upper extremities affected to a lesser
degree.

1. During gait, the combination of excessive hip flexion,
adduction, and internal rotation produces a scissoring
pattern. This type of gait deviation results primarily due
to the presence of spasticity. Common management
techniques of spasticity in this population include muscle
lengthening or release of the musculature. Skeletal muscle
relaxants are also used to control spasticity and can be
given orally, intrathecally or through injection at the motor
point.

2. The knee flexors, not the knee extensors, are shortened
secondary to the spasticity associated with cerebral palsy.

3. The hip adductors, not the hip abductors, are shortened
secondary to the spasticity associated with cerebral palsy.

4. The plantar flexors, not the dorsiflexors, are shortened
secondary to the spasticity associated with cerebral palsy.
Ankle-foot orthoses are commonly used to position the foot
in dorsiflexion to facilitate foot clearance during the swing
phase of gait or to decrease genu recurvatum.
