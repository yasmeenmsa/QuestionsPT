PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 131
What is the MOST likely rationale for the child’s significant gross
motor delays?

1. Congenital heart defects

2. Seizure disorder

3. Atlantoaxial instability

4. Obesity

Correct Answer: 1 (Goodman Pathology p. 1128)

Congenital heart defects are common in infants with Down

syndrome. The presence of a congenital heart defect can delay

gross motor development well beyond the 6-12 month delay
already associated with Down syndrome.

1. Congenital heart defects are reported to occur in up to
two-thirds of infants with Down syndrome. infant
with Down syndrome and a congenital heart defect may
require additional hospitalization or surgeries which
impact their gross motor development. This child has
history of Tetralogy of Fallot, which is a congenital heart
defect requiring surgical repair. Furthermore, the infant
required additional corrective surgery seven months later.
It is extremely likely that congenital heart defects are
contributing to the child’s gross motor delay.

2. Seizures have the potential to impact gross motor
development. However, they are uncommon in children with
Down syndrome.

3. Atlantoaxial instability can be present in approximately 15
percent of children with Down syndrome. The majority of
those affected by atlantoaxial instability are asymptomatic,
and as a result, gross motor development is not typically
impacted.

4. Obesity alone does not significantly impact gross motor
development, but it is extremely common in children and
adults with Down syndrome, often due to a sedentary
lifestyle.

PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 132
Which of the following therapeutic activities would be the MOST
appropriate to improve this child’s gross motor skills?

1. Incorporate play activities (e.g., skipping, hopping, jumping)
to improve coordination

2. Utilize inhibitory techniques in order to decrease hypertonicity
of the trunk

3. Apply stretching techniques to tight muscles to prevent
contractures

4. Utilize trunk rotation during play in order to elicit postural
reactions

Correct Answer: 4 (Goodman Pathology p. 1130)

Treatment should emphasize stability and progress to mobility

exercises to improve independent standing (stability) and then

independent walking (upright mobility).

1. Play activities are desirable to improve gross motor skills.
However, the child’s current clinical presentation (e.g.,
postural instability, tonal abnormalities) would make these
types of activities unrealistic.

2. Individuals with Down syndrome tend to be hypotonic and
therefore techniques to decrease hypertonicity would not be
appropriate.

3. Stretching techniques would not be emphasized since laxity
and weakness are consistent with the clinical presentation of
Down syndrome.

4. Integrating trunk rotation into play activities would be an
effective strategy to improve motor control in patients
with Down syndrome. Development of postural reactions is
necessary for stability in upright standing and progression
to independent walking.
