PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 130

What is the MOST likely rationale for the physical therapist
instructing the parents to avoid having the child perform forward
rolls in the community gym class?

1. Increased risk for hydrocephalus

2. Increased risk for spinal cord injury

3. Increased risk for traumatic brain injury

4. Increased risk for developmental delay

Correct Answer: 2 (Goodman Pathology p. 1130)

Patients diagnosed with Down syndrome are typically hypotonic

and often present with vertebral instability due to ligamentous

laxity, especially at the atlantoaxial joint (Cl-C2). The described
activity would significantly increase the risk for atlantoaxial joint
subluxation or dislocation and should be avoided in these patients.

1. Hydrocephalus refers to the buildup of fluid in the ventricles
deep within the brain. The excess fluid increases the size of
the ventricles and places pressure on the brain. Attempting
a forward roll would not increase the risk of hydrocephalus.

2. Dislocation of the atlantoaxial joint is considered a medical
emergency since it can place abnormal compression on the
spinal cord. Patients with vertebral instability in this area
should avoid activities such as diving, tumbling, and contact
sports to minimize the risk for these life-threatening injuries.

3. Though the head is coming in contact with the ground during
a forward roll, it would be unlikely to cause a traumatic brain
injury. A patient with Down syndrome would not be at any
greater risk for a traumatic brain injury when compared to
other patients performing this same movement.

4. Developmental delay is a component of the clinical presenta-
tion for patients with Down syndrome. However, performing
a forward roll would not further increase the risk of develop-
mental delay in these patients.
