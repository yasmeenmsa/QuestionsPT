PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 87
What imaging technique is MOST appropriate to confirm the
patient’s medical diagnosis?

1. Fluoroscopy

2. Myelography

3. Arthrography

4. Computed tomography

Correct Answer: 3 (Magee p. 58)

Adhesive capsulitis is an enigmatic shoulder disorder characterized

by inflammation and fibrotic thickening of the anterior and inferior

joint capsule of the shoulder. A decrease in space within the joint
capsule leads to a decrease in synovial fluid and further irritation
to the glenohumeral joint.

1. Fluoroscopy is designed to show motion within the body
utilizing x-ray imaging and injection of a contrast dye. Due
to the higher dose of radiation with fluoroscopy and the
ability to detect joint motion restrictions on clinical exam
with adhesive capsulitis, fluoroscopy is not a likely choice to
confirm the diagnosis.

2. Myelography is an invasive test that combines fluoroscopy
and radiography to evaluate the spinal subarachnoid space.
The test utilizes a contrast medium that is injected into the
epidural space by spinal puncture. Myelography is used to
identify bone displacement, disk herniation, spinal cord
compression or tumors.

3. Arthrography is an invasive procedure that uses x-ray
imaging and an injected contrast dye to visualize joint
structures. An arthrogram can assist with the diagnosis of
adhesive capsulitis by detecting decreased fluid volume
within the joint capsule.

4. Computed tomography is a non-invasive imaging procedure
in which x-ray images are taken from multiple angles using
large circular scanner. Computed tomography scans are most
commonly used to diagnose spinal lesions and in diagnostic
studies of the brain.
