PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 92

A physical therapist performs autolytic debridement in an attempt
to remove nonviable tissue from a stage 4 pressure injury. Autolytic
debridement removes necrotic tissue from the wound by using
which of the following methods?

1. instrument

2. applied force

3. The body’s own mechanisms

4. Acommercially prepared enzyme

Correct Answer: 3 (Sussman p. 445) =a

Autolytic debridement is typically performed using a moisture-

retentive dressing. The dressing maintains a moist wound

environment which promotes rehydration of viable tissue and
allows the body’s enzymes to digest necrotic tissue.

1. Sharp debridement requires the use of scalpel, scissors, and/
or forceps to selectively remove nonviable tissue, foreign
material or debris from a wound.

2. Wound irrigation removes nonviable tissue from the wound
bed using pressurized fluid which serves as an externally
applied force. Pulsatile lavage is an example of a specific
wound irrigation technique.

3. Autolytic debridement refers to using the body’s own
mechanisms to remove nonviable tissue. Common methods
of autolytic debridement include transparent films, hydro-
colloids, hydrogels, and alginates.

4. Enzymatic debridement requires the application of
commercially prepared enzyme to the surface of nonviable
tissue. The applied enzyme attempts to degrade the
nonviable tissue through gradual digestion.

PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 93

A physical therapist completes an

assessment procedure over several lung

segments as depicted in the video. The

therapist classifies the sounds as tympanic.

Which condition would MOST likely be

associated with this finding?

1. Atelectasis

2. Lung consolidation

3. Pneumothorax

4. Pulmonary embolism

Mediate percussion is the act of tapping the surface of the body to
identify areas of altered density. By analyzing the sound produced,
the therapist can determine whether the underlying structure is
air-filled, fluid-filled or solid. Tympany is characterized by hollow
sounds vaguely resembling a drumbeat. It is almost exclusively
associated with a large pneumothorax.

1. Atelectasis is the absence of gas in part or all of a lung due
to a collapse of the lung tissue. The condition is most often
associated with decreased breath sounds and increased
fremitus. Atelectasis would be more likely to result in a flat or
dull sound with percussion.

2. Lung consolidation refers to an area of the lung that is filled
with fluid. The fluid may be edema, inflammatory exudate,
pus, water or blood. The condition is most often associated
with decreased breath sounds and increased fremitus on the
side of the consolidation. Lung consolidation would be more
likely to result in a flat or dull sound with mediate percussion.

3. Pneumothorax refers to an abnormal collection of air in
the pleural cavity which may or may not be considered
emergent depending on its characteristics (e.g., size,
etiology). Pressure from the pneumothorax impedes the
lung’s ability to inflate, causing chest pain with attempts to
do so. A pneumothorax would result in a tympanic sound
with mediate percussion.

4. Pulmonary embolism is a condition where one or more
arteries in the lungs become blocked. In most cases,
pulmonary embolism is caused by blood clots from the lower
extremities. Symptoms can vary greatly, but may include
shortness of breath, chest pain that worsens with deep
breathing, coughing up bloody or blood-streaked sputum,
and a rapid or irregular pulse. The presence of pulmonary
embolism would not produce tympanic sounds with mediate
percussion.
