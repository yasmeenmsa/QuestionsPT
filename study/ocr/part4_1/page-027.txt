PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 44
Which of the following options BEST explains the diminished passive
knee flexion range of motion when measured in prone?

1. Active insufficiency of the knee extensors
2. Active insufficiency of the knee flexors
3. Passive insufficiency of the knee extensors
4. Passive insufficiency of the knee flexors
Correct Answer: 3 (Kisner p. 65)
Passive insufficiency occurs when a two-joint muscle is incapable
of lengthening to the extent required to allow full range of motion
of all joints it crosses simultaneously. In a prone position, the hip
is ina relative position of extension. As a result, the two-joint knee
extensors are lengthened which effectively limits knee flexion in
this position.

1. Active insufficiency occurs with active movement and not
passive movement. The question specifically asks about
passive knee flexion.

2. Active insufficiency occurs with active movement and not
passive movement.

3. Passive insufficiency refers to a lack of muscle length.
When performing passive knee flexion, the two-joint
knee extensors are placed on stretch. In the presence of
insufficient length, this may contribute to a limitation in
knee flexion when measured in prone.

4. When performing passive knee flexion, the knee flexors
would shorten and therefore would not limit knee flexion
range of motion.

PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 45
Which of the following factors would have the GREATEST influence
on the patient’s candidacy for surgery?

1. Anthropometric measurements

2. Hamstrings/quadriceps strength ratio

3. Skeletal maturity

4. Somatotype

Correct Answer: 3 (Hertling p. 518)

Physical therapists should possess a general idea of how specific

factors, such as normal growth and development, influence

candidate’s eligibility for selected medical and surgical procedures.

1. Common anthropometric measurements used for adults
include height, weight, body mass index (BMI), waist-to-hip
ratio, and percentage of body fat. These measures are then
compared to reference standards to assess weight status and
the risk for various diseases.

2. Hamstrings/quadriceps strength ratio is a general measure
of the relative strength of the hamstrings compared to the
relative strength of the quadriceps. Strength is an important
factor both prior to and post-surgery. However, it is unlikely
that this would influence candidacy for surgery.

3. Due to the potential impact on future bone growth
for the 13-year-old, lack of skeletal maturity can be
contraindication to anterior cruciate ligament reconstructive
surgery. X-rays are used to assess the tibial and femoral
growth plates, which can provide information on the extent
of skeletal maturity.

4. Somatotype is a term used to classify body type. The most
common classifications of somatotype include endomorph,
mesomorph, and ectomorph.
