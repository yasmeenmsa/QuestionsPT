PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 134
Which lumbar mechanical spinal traction parameters would be MOST
appropriate to utilize during the initial treatment?

1. Supine with the legs extended using 40 pounds of force

2. Supine with the legs extended using 80 pounds of force

3. Supine with the hips and knees flexed and the lower legs
resting on a stool using 40 pounds of force

4. Supine with the hips and knees flexed and the lower legs
resting on a stool using 80 pounds of force

Correct Answer: 4 (Cameron p. 428)

Mechanical traction is a modality that applies forces to the body

to separate joint surfaces and decrease pressure. Mechanical

traction is indicated for many diagnoses and allows for variation
and adjustment of the established protocol based on individual
patient needs.

1. A supine position with the legs extended is appropriate for
mechanical traction but is not ideal when treating spinal
stenosis since a flexed position more effectively widens
the intervertebral foramen. Forty pounds of traction force
would also be insufficient to cause actual separation of the
vertebrae in a 200-pound individual.

2. A supine position with the legs extended is appropriate for
mechanical traction but is not ideal when treating spinal
stenosis since a flexed position more effectively widens the
intervertebral foramen. Eighty pounds of traction force would
correspond to 40 percent of the individual’s body weight and
is therefore a more desirable amount of force. Fifty percent
of body weight is often used as a gross approximation of
the amount of force required for actual separation of the
vertebrae.

3. Asupine position with the hips and knees flexed is appropriate
for mechanical traction when treating spinal stenosis since
the flexed position effectively widens the intervertebral
foramen. Forty pounds of traction force would be insufficient
to cause actual separation of the vertebrae in a 200-pound
individual.

4. A supine position with the hips and knees flexed is
appropriate for mechanical traction when treating spinal
stenosis since the flexed position effectively widens the
intervertebral foramen. Eighty pounds of traction force
would correspond to 40 percent of the individual’s body
weight and is therefore a more desirable amount of force.
Fifty percent of body weight is often used as a gross
approximation of the amount of force required for actual
separation of the vertebrae.

PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 135
Which medical treatment option would be MOST appropriate if
physical therapy was determined to be ineffective following four
weeks of treatment?

1. Foraminotomy
2. Steroid injection
3. Laminotomy
4. Laminectomy
Correct Answer: 2 (Dutton p. 1361)
Medical/nonsurgical treatment options for spinal stenosis include
medications (e.g., NSAIDs, analgesics), physical therapy, exercise,
epidural steroid injections, and facet nerve blocks. Surgical
treatment options include laminectomy, laminotomy, laminoplasty,
and endoscopic discectomy. Conservative interventions would
likely be warranted in the presented scenario given that the
stenosis is relatively new (three months) and an MRI classified
the stenosis as “moderate.” In addition, the patient is still able to
engage in exercise activities, although less often.

1. A foraminotomy is a surgical procedure that enlarges the
foraminal area, which decreases pressure on compressed
nerves. This type of surgical procedure would typically not
occur until more conservative measures have failed.

2. Epidural corticosteroid injections place medication into
the epidural space. The medication used is a combination
of corticosteroids and a local anesthetic, which together
reduce inflammation in the spinal canal and relieve pain.
Corticosteroids are strong anti-inflammatory medications
and, when placed into the epidural space, can significantly
reduce inflammation around an irritated nerve that is
causing back and leg pain and discomfort.

3. A laminotomy removes only a portion of the lamina of
vertebral arch to relieve pressure on the vertebral canal.
laminotomy is less invasive than conventional spinal surgeries
such as a laminectomy. This type of surgical procedure would
typically not occur until more conservative measures have
failed.

4. A complete laminectomy involves the removal of the entire
lamina, the spinous process, and the associated ligamentum
flavum. A laminectomy is usually performed in the presence
of disk protrusion or spinal stenosis. This type of surgical
procedure would typically not occur until more conservative
measures have failed.
