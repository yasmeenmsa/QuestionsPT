PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 152
A physical therapist attempts to stimulate
a primitive reflex as shown in the video. af
Which functional activity would be MOST
negatively influenced by the abnormal
persistence of this reflex?

1. Eye-hand coordination

2. Feeding

3. Weight bearing on an open hand
for propping

4. Midline use of hands
Correct Answer: 3 (Palisano p. 54)
Primitive reflexes are normally present during infancy and become
integrated by the central nervous system at an early age. The
palmar grasp reflex is elicited by placing an object (e.g., a finger)
across the infant’s palm. If the reflex is present, the infant’s fingers
will flex around the object. The normal age of the response is birth
to four months.

1. Abnormal persistence of the palmar grasp reflex would not
significantly impair eye-hand coordination. This would be
more negatively influenced by the Moro and startle reflex.

2. Abnormal persistence of the palmar grasp reflex would not
significantly impair feeding. This would be more negatively
influenced by failure to integrate the asymmetrical tonic neck
reflex.

3. Surface contact with an open palm would result in an infant
reflexively closing their fist. This action would make it
extremely difficult or potentially impossible to bear weight
through an open palm.

4. Abnormal persistence of the palmar grasp reflex would not
significantly impair midline use of the hands. This would
be more negatively influenced by failure to integrate the
asymmetrical tonic neck reflex.

PTEXAM ONE: QUESTION 153
A physical therapist obtains an x-ray of
a patient recently referred to physical
therapy after experiencing an increase in
back pain following activity. The patient
previously participated in competitive
gymnastics, however, reports that their
back was unable to tolerate the intensity
of training. Based on the presented
x-ray, the therapist should expect the
patient’s medical diagnosis to be which
of the following clinical conditions?

1. Spondylitis

2. Spondylolysis

3. Spondylolisthesis

4. Spondyloptosis

Correct Answer: 3 (Magee p. 645) =C

There are a variety of commonly encountered medical conditions

that significantly impact the lumbar spine. Physical therapists

should be familiar with the clinical presentation and management
of these medical conditions.

1. Spondylitis refers to inflammation of a vertebra.

2. Spondylolysis refers to a defect in the pars interarticularis
or the arch of the vertebra. This is most common in the L5
vertebra, but can also occur in other lumbar or thoracic
vertebrae.

3. Spondylolisthesis refers to the forward displacement of one
vertebra over another. The x-ray involves spondylolisthesis
at the L5-S1 level. Individuals involved in_ physical
activities such as weight lifting, gymnastics or football are
particularly susceptible to this condition. The severity of the
spondylolisthesis is classified on a scale of 1-5 based on how
much a given vertebral body has slipped forward over the
vertebral body beneath it.

4. Spondyloptosis refers to the condition where a vertebral
body has shifted completely off of the adjacent vertebral
body (grade 5).
