Unit 3 Examinations Answer
PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 44
Which goal would be the MOST appropriate if the patient
participates in an aquatic exercise program?

1. Reduce the severity and frequency of exacerbations

2. Improve the patient’s fine motor coordination

3. Improve the patient’s level of fitness

4. Improve the patient’s balance when walking on uneven
surfaces

Correct Answer: 3 (Lazaro p. 525)

The patient’s present functional status would make it difficult

to perform many traditional exercise programs. The aquatic

environment can support the patient’s movement while working
on mobility. Working toward a fitness goal may have a positive
impact on quality of life.

1. Therapeutic exercise will not affect the course of the
patient’s disease, including the severity and frequency of
exacerbations. However, it can be beneficial in addressing
impairments (e.g., fatigue, weakness) associated with the
disease.

2. While fine motor coordination may be an impairment
associated with multiple sclerosis, performing exercises in
pool would not address this impairment.

3. Therapeutic exercise has the potential to improve fitness
levels in patients with multiple sclerosis. Walking and
performing active exercises in the pool is an appropriate
way to improve this patient’s level of fitness.

4. While poor balance when walking on uneven surfaces
may be an impairment associated with multiple sclerosis,
performing exercises in a pool would not specifically address
this impairment. It would be more appropriate to address this
impairment by practicing in a context-specific environment
(i.e., walking on uneven surfaces). Multiple sclerosis is
degenerative disease and based on patient’s symptoms, the
goal would be to provide appropriate assistive devices for
mobility instead of focusing on remediating balance.

PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 45
After approximately 25 minutes of a 30 minute therapy session
focusing on mobility and activities of daily living, the patient
reports feeling extremely fatigued. The therapist promptly takes the
patient’s vital signs and concludes they are unremarkable. What is
the MOST appropriate therapist action?
1. Discontinue the treatment session
2. Modify the intensity of the current therapy session
3. Complete a formal reassessment of the patient
4. Contact the referring physician to discuss the patient’s
condition
Correct Answer: 1 (Lazaro p. 525)
Most patients with multiple sclerosis report experiencing fatigue,
with greater than 50% of individuals reporting it as their worst
symptom. Physical therapists should be vigilant to identify signs of
fatigue in this population due to the potential debilitating effects.
1. Patients with multiple sclerosis are extremely susceptible
to exercise-related fatigue, heat intolerance, and falling.
The patient clearly described their status as “extremely
fatigued.” In addition, the patient has already engaged in 25
minutes of physical therapy. Without additional information
related to the patient’s typical exercise tolerance and the
type of multiple sclerosis relapsing-remitting, primary
progressive, secondary progressive), it is necessary to be
conservative and discontinue the current session.
2. While decreasing the intensity would reduce the workload,
the patient has already reached a point of extreme fatigue.
At this point, it is more important that the patient avoid
overexertion to avoid injury and exacerbation of symptoms.
3. Fatigue is a common symptom in patients with multiple
sclerosis. A formal reassessment is unnecessary.
4. Fatigue is a common symptom in patients with multiple
sclerosis. Contacting the patient’s physician is unnecessary.
