PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 7
A patient performs an exercise
as depicted in the video. Which
modification to the exercise
would be the MOST appropriate
to assist with regaining knee
hyperextension?

1. Reposition the patient in
long sitting

2. Increase the force of the muscle contraction
3. Place towel under the ankle
4. Add weight to the distal femur
Correct Answer: 3 (Dunleavy p. 252)
The patient in the video is performing a quad set, which is
characterized by an isometric contraction of the quadriceps in
supine by pushing the knee downward into a towel. In an isometric
exercise, muscular force is generated without a change in muscle
length. Isometric exercises are often performed against an
immovable object. Submaximal isometric exercises are traditionally
used in rehabilitation programs, especially in the early phases.

1. Repositioning the patient in long sitting while the patient
performs the quad set exercise would place the hamstrings in
lengthened position. This positioning would likely create passive
insufficiency of the hamstrings if they were tight or active
insufficiency of the rectus femoris due to muscle activation in
a maximally shortened position. The described repositioning
would not assist with regaining knee hyperextension.

2. Increasing the force of the muscle contraction might help with
strength gains at that point in the range of motion. However,
continuing the quad set exercise in the same position as
described would not assist with regaining hyperextension
due to the positioning of the towel under the posterior
surface of the knee.

3. Placing a rolled towel under the ankle elevates the knee
and calf from the treatment table and is the most likely
modification to improve knee extension
hyperextension when appropriate. The size of the towel roll
under the ankle can be controlled to adjust the amount of
knee hyperextension allowed during the quad set exercise.

4. Adding a cuff weight to the distal femur may help to
improve knee extension mobility in a patient that has
flexion contracture. However, it would not help improve
hyperextension mobility, especially since there is still
towel roll under the knee keeping the patient’s knee in slight
flexion. A more appropriate modification for regaining knee
hyperextension would be to place a rolled towel under the
ankle while performing the exercise.

PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 8
A patient being treated for low back pain indicates that they
recently were diagnosed with benign prostatic hyperplasia. Which
of the following symptoms is MOST commonly associated with this
condition?

1. Epigastric pain

2. Painful urination

3. Painful ejaculation

4. Urge to urinate frequently

Correct Answer: 4 (Goodman Differential Diagnosis

p. 398)

Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is an enlargement of the

prostate that commonly occurs in men over 50 years old. The

enlargement of the prostate squeezes the urethra and interferes
with urinary function and, less frequently, sexual function.

BPH is more commonly associated with lower abdominal,
low back or thigh pain, not epigastric pain.

2. Though a patient with BPH may experience urinary problems,
painful urination is not typically one of the symptoms. Painful
urination is more likely with conditions such as prostatitis.

3. with BPH may experience sexual dysfunction
(e.g., difficulty attaining an erection), painful ejaculation
is not typically one of the symptoms. Painful ejaculation is
more likely with conditions such as prostatitis.

4. Patients with BPH typically have issues passing urine due
to the enlargement of the prostate and its position next to
the urethra. Symptoms include hesitancy of urination, small
amounts of urine when voiding, dribbling at the end of
urination, urge to urinate frequently, and nocturia.
