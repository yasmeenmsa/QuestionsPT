PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 42
Which of the following BEST characterizes the patient’s gait?

1. Wide base of support with exaggerated movements

2. Legs crossing the midline

3. Small shuffling steps and festination

4. High stepping with foot slap

Correct Answer: 1 (Magee p. 1118)

An ataxic gait is often caused by damage to the cerebellum, which

controls muscular coordination, though this gait pattern can result

from other causes as well (e.g., alcohol intoxication, stroke).

1. Ataxiais defined by the presence of abnormal, uncoordinated
movements. An ataxic gait pattern is characterized by
staggering and unsteadiness. There is usually a wide base
of support and movements are exaggerated.

2. Ascissoring gait pattern is characterized by the legs crossing
the midline upon advancement and is more commonly
associated with spastic cerebral palsy.

3. A Parkinsonian gait pattern is characterized by increased
forward flexion of the trunk and knees, shuffling with quick
and small steps, and festination.

4. Asteppage gait pattern is characterized by high steps where
the hips and knees are moved to excessive heights due to
dorsiflexor weakness, with foot slap occurring after initial
contact.

PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 43
Which pharmacological agent would MOST limit the patient’s ability
to complete a sit to stand transfer?

1. Calcium carbonate

2. Dantrolene sodium

3. Levodopa

4. Secobarbital

Correct Answer: 2 (Lazaro p. 526)

Physical therapists must possess an awareness of commonly

used pharmacological agents, their indications, and potential

side effects. This awareness must extend to the unique clinical
presentation of each patient and the established physical therapy
plan of care.

Calcium carbonate is a bone mineral regulating agent
that attempts to enhance and maximize bone mass along
with preventing bone loss or decreasing the rate of bone
resorption. Side effects of this medication include nausea,
decreased appetite, constipation, and dry mouth. This
pharmacological agent would not have a significant impact
on the patient’s ability to complete the transfer.

2. Dantrolene sodium is a post-synaptic muscle relaxant
that exerts its effects directly on the skeletal muscle cells.
Side effects include drowsiness, confusion, dizziness, and
generalized muscle weakness. Reducing the patient’s
spasticity may significantly impact their ability to complete
the transfer since it will likely reduce the patient’s extensor
tone which is presently being relied upon to complete the
transfer.

3. Levodopa is a dopamine replacement agent that assists to
reduce the symptoms of Parkinson’s disease. Side effects of
this medication include arrhythmias, gastrointestinal distress,
orthostatic hypotension, and dyskinesias. The potential for
orthostatic hypotension and dyskinesias could impact the
patient’s ability to complete the transfer, but it is unlikely that
this impact would be as significant as the reduced extensor
tone associated with the use of a muscle relaxant.

4. Secobarbital is an antiepileptic agent that reduces or
eliminates seizure activity within the brain. These agents
attempt to inhibit the firing of certain cerebral neurons within
the central nervous system. Side effects of this medication
include drowsiness, dizziness, and ataxia. This medication
would not have a significant impact on the patient’s ability to
complete the transfer.
