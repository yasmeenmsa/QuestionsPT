PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 3

A physical therapist assesses a patient’s

pulse as depicted in the video. This pulse
is bordered by which of the following
structures?

1. Flexor digitorum profundus and flexor
pollicis longus

2. Flexor digitorum superficialis and
flexor carpi ulnaris

3. Extensor digitorum and extensor
carpi ulnaris

4. Extensor carpi ulnaris and extensor pollicis longus

Correct Answer: 2 (Hoppenfeld p. 81)

Palpation of pulses is an examination procedure often used to

assess a patient’s circulatory status. Therapists should be familiar

with the most desirable locations to palpate common peripheral
pulses.

1. The flexor digitorum profundus and flexor pollicis longus
are located on the volar surface of the forearm, but do not
border the ulnar artery.

2. The ulnar artery is located on the palmar surface of the
medial wrist. It is located between the flexor digitorum
superficialis and flexor carpi ulnaris tendons.

3. The ulnar artery is assessed with the therapist’s fingers
positioned on the volar surface of the forearm and not the
dorsal surface. The extensor digitorum and extensor carpi
ulnaris muscles are located on the dorsal side of the forearm.

4. The ulnar artery is assessed with the therapist’s fingers
positioned on the volar surface of the forearm and not
the dorsal surface. The extensor carpi ulnaris and extensor
pollicis longus are located on the dorsal side of the forearm.

PTEXAM TWO: QUESTION 4
A physical therapist receives a referral for a patient diagnosed
with systemic lupus erythematosus. Which of the following patient
profiles would be MOST consistent with the onset of this medical
diagnosis?

1. female
2. A 67-year-old female
3. male
4. A 61-year-old male
Correct Answer: 1 (Goodman Pathology p. 282) =a
Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is a connective tissue disorder
caused by an autoimmune reaction in the body. The primary
manifestation of the condition is the production of destructive
antibodies that are directed at the individual’s own body. The
chronic inflammatory disorder produces a variety of symptoms
depending on the severity and extent of involvement. SLE can
occur at any age, however, is most common during childbearing
years. The disorder is more common in women than men.

1. SLE is more common in females and a 29-year-old is within
the period of peak incidence (i.e., 15-40 years of age).

2. SLE is more common in females, however, a 67-year-old
female is significantly older than the period of peak incidence
(i.e., 15-40 years of age).

3. SLE is less common in males. A 27-year-old is within the
period of peak incidence (i.e., 15-40 years of age), however,
the fact that the individual is a male makes this answer less
likely to be consistent with the established risk profile.

4. SLE is less common in males. In addition, a 61-year-old is
significantly older than the period of peak incidence (i.e., 15-
4O years of age).
