### Question 1

A physical therapist attempts to assess a primitive reflex as depicted in the video. Which reflex is the therapist attempting to stimulate?

1. Plantar grasp
2. Moro
3. Landau
4. Galant

**Correct answer: 4**

**Explanation**

Primitive reflexes are normally present during infancy and eventually become integrated by the central nervous system. Integration of a primitive reflex refers to a reflex that is no longer present despite an appropriate stimulus. Each primitive reflex has an established timeline for when it is normally integrated.

1. The plantar grasp reflex is stimulated by placing pressure on the ball of the foot, generally in standing. The response is flexion and curling of the toes. The normal age of the response is from 28 weeks of gestation to nine months.

2. The Moro reflex is stimulated when an infant's head is suddenly allowed to fall into extension. The action causes "startled look" followed by upper extremity abduction with the fingers open, then crossing the trunk into adduction. The normal age of the response is from 28 weeks of gestation to five months. The Moro reflex is different than the startle reflex.

3. The Landau reflex is an equilibrium response that occurs when a child responds to prone suspension by aligning their head and extremities in line with the plane of the body. Although the response begins around three months of age, it is not fully integrated until the child's second year.

4. The Galant reflex is stimulated by touching the skin along the spine from the shoulder to the hip. The response is lateral flexion of the trunk to the side of the stimulus. The normal age of the response is from 30 weeks of gestation to two months.
